## Language and tone

We write in a clear, simple and courteous way, without bureaucratic jargon, and we expect the same from people who take part. Staff who reply for the municipality are helpful and reply without delay, but they do not anticipate the outcome of a case and do not make commitments outside the cases allowed (Decree of the President of the Republic No. 62 of 16 April 2013, as amended by Presidential Decree No. 81 of 13 June 2023, art. 12, paragraphs 1 and 4). Matters concerning your case or your personal data are not dealt with in public conversations: we ask you to move to a private channel, for example your personal area or the PEC (same decree, art. 11-ter, paragraph 3, and art. 12, paragraph 5). The opinions that employees express on their personal profiles are their own and do not represent the municipality (art. 11-ter, paragraph 1). The conduct of people who work for the municipality on social networks is governed by the municipality's code of conduct: [[title and address of the municipality's code of conduct and, if there is one, of its social media policy]].

## Participation and public content

To write a comment under a news item or an event you need an account; people without an account can only read. Next to each comment the profile name of the person who wrote it is shown: choose a name that does not reveal more than you want. Comments are published at once, without a check before publication, and are checked afterwards, as the section on moderation explains.

A report you send is not public at once: it starts with the status “Pending” and is visible only to you and to the operators. A moderator checks it. If the moderator accepts it, it becomes visible to everyone with the status “In Review” or a later status; if the moderator rejects it, it stays non-public and the reason is recorded in its history, which you can see. When a report is public, everyone can see the title, the description, the type, the address and the position on the map, the status, the history of statuses and the day it was sent (not the time). We do not show your name or your email on the report page; however, if you write a comment, your profile name appears next to the comment. Under a public report everyone can read the comments, but only the author of the report and authorised staff of the municipality can write them.

The photos and files you attach to a report are for the operators and are not shown on the public page of the report, but they may be accessible to anyone who knows their address: do not attach anything you would not want to be public.

## Participation rules and forbidden behaviour

Take part respectfully and stay on topic. The following are forbidden:

- insults, threats, harassment and attacks on the reputation of people or bodies (defamation: Criminal Code, art. 595);
- content that spreads hatred or incites discrimination or violence on racial, ethnic, national or religious grounds (Criminal Code, art. 604-bis);
- any other unlawful content, or content that violates the rights of other people;
- personal data of other people, such as names, phone numbers, addresses, vehicle plates and recognisable faces, and special categories of data such as data about health or opinions;
- false, invented or repeated reports sent to disturb the service;
- advertising, chain messages and messages repeated in series (spam);
- using a false identity and passing yourself off as the municipality, as one of its employees or as another person;
- links to dangerous or deceptive sites and harmful files;
- using automated programs to send messages: to prevent abuse the site limits the comments that can be sent in one minute.

People who do not follow the rules may have their content hidden or removed and, if they repeat, lose access to the service (see the [legal notices](note-legali)).

## Moderation and removal of content

For users' contributions the municipality follows procedures consistent with Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 on digital services (arts. 14, 16 and 17). Comments are published at once and checked afterwards: the municipality does not read them all beforehand and has no general obligation to monitor users' content (art. 8 of the same regulation), but it steps in when it finds content itself or receives a notice. Citizens' reports, instead, are checked by a moderator before they become public.

The municipality may edit content to remove other people's personal data or forbidden parts, hide it or remove it, and limit or close the account of anyone who breaks the rules more than once. It applies these rules diligently, objectively and proportionately, taking into account everyone's freedom of expression (art. 14, paragraph 4). A comment is not removed just because it criticises the municipality or the service, if it follows the rules.

When it hides or removes content, or closes an account, and we have your contact details, the municipality explains clearly what it decided, which facts and which rule or law it applied, whether the decision comes from a notice and how to challenge it (art. 17, paragraphs 1 and 3). When one of your reports is rejected, the reason is recorded in its history. The municipality keeps removed content and moderation records for [[retention period for removed content and moderation records]].

## Reporting content or challenging a decision

If you find a comment, a report or an image that you think is unlawful or against these rules, write to [[email or PEC address for reporting unlawful or harmful content]]. To help us decide, state: why you think the content is unlawful; the exact address (URL) of the page; your name and your email, which are not needed for content concerning child sexual abuse; a statement confirming in good faith that the information is accurate and complete (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, art. 16, paragraph 2). If you leave contact details, we confirm receipt without undue delay and tell you our decision with the available means of redress (art. 16, paragraphs 4 and 5). We handle notices in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary and objective manner (art. 16, paragraph 6): [[time within which the municipality decides on notices about content]].

If you disagree with a decision, write to the same address: it is reviewed by [[office or person who reviews moderation decisions]]. Going to court and, for personal data, lodging a complaint with the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (the Italian data protection authority) always remain possible.

If a minor aged at least fourteen, or the minor's parent, is the victim of cyberbullying, they may ask for the minor's data published on the site to be blacked out, removed or blocked, stating the address of each item. The law provides that the site operator must say within 24 hours that it has taken up the request and act within 48 hours; otherwise the person may turn to the Garante (Law No. 71 of 29 May 2017, art. 2).

To have your personal data deleted or to exercise your other rights, see the [privacy notice](privacy): we reply within one month (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, art. 12, paragraph 3) and deletion is subject to the limits of art. 17, paragraph 3.

## Photos, images and other people's data

The photos you attach to a report may be JPEG, PNG or PDF, up to 5 files of 10 MB each. Photograph the problem, not people: avoid recognisable faces, vehicle plates and documents. A person's portrait may not be displayed, reproduced or put on the market without that person's consent, except for the exceptions provided by law, which in any case do not apply if publication harms the honour, reputation or dignity of the person (Law No. 633 of 22 April 1941, arts. 96 and 97; Civil Code, art. 10). If a recognisable person appears in a photo you sent, you are responsible for it.

Do not publish recognisable images of minors. The Garante per la protezione dei dati personali advises against publishing photos of minors and, if they are published, making the face unrecognisable. Photos of people that the municipality publishes in news items and events follow this rule: [[how the municipality obtains consent to publish images of the people shown and of minors, and when it relies on the exceptions provided by law]].

Photos taken with a smartphone may contain hidden data, such as the location or the time of the shot: if you do not want to share it, remove it before uploading the photo.

If you appear in a photo published on the site and do not want it to stay online, write to the address given in the section “Reporting content or challenging a decision”: we assess the request at once and, if there is no basis that justifies publication, we remove the photo without delay.

## Copyright, content licence and reuse

Send only texts and images that you wrote or took, or that you have permission to use: photographs are protected by copyright, which belongs to the photographer (Law No. 633 of 22 April 1941, art. 88). The rights in the content you send remain yours. By sending it, you grant the municipality a non-exclusive, free licence to use, keep and publish it to run the service and for [[other purposes and duration of the licence granted by users]]. You may quote short parts of the site's texts for criticism or discussion, stating the source, within the limits of the law (same law, art. 70, paragraph 1).

Content published by the municipality may be reused under [[licence for the municipality's content and open data, for example CC BY 4.0]], citing the source with the wording [[attribution wording required by the municipality]]. Data and documents published without an express licence are open data, unless personal data is disclosed (Legislative Decree No. 82 of 7 March 2005, art. 52, paragraph 2). Third-party images and texts on the site remain with their respective owners, under the conditions given in the [legal notices](note-legali).

## Logo and coat of arms of the municipality

The name, logo and coat of arms of the municipality identify the administration. They may not be used without authorisation, nor altered, nor placed next to private, commercial or political messages, nor used in a way that suggests an official site, profile or initiative of the municipality or one it sponsors. Coats of arms and other symbols of public interest cannot be registered as a trade mark without the authorisation of the competent authority (Legislative Decree No. 30 of 10 February 2005, art. 10, paragraph 1). To ask for authorisation write to [[office and address to ask for use of the logo and coat of arms]]; the rules of use are in [[municipal regulation or resolution on the use of the coat of arms and logo]]. If you find a profile or a site that uses the logo or coat of arms and is not in the list of official channels, let us know at the address for notices given above.
